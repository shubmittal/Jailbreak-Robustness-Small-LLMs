\section{Confirmatory hypotheses}

These three hypotheses are registered as confirmatory. Section~6 of the paper
reports the observed direction and magnitude of each.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{H1 (judge dependence).} Under the unified protocol, the safety
(ASR) ranking of the four models is \textbf{not} stable across the three judges:
at least one pairwise model ordering flips between the HarmBench fine-tuned
classifier and Llama-Guard-3-1B per the ranking-flip rule in \S5.7.
\item \textbf{H2 (defense-cost spread).} The ${\sim}60$-word primary defensive
system prompt reduces ASR on \textbf{all four} models, but the per-model FRR
penalty is \textbf{non-uniform}: the spread of the defense-cost ratio $C(M)$
across the four models exceeds a factor of two (corroborated by the area metric
$A(M)$).
\item \textbf{H3 (capability-vs-alignment).} Attack-family vulnerability is
differentiated in interpretable ways: encoding/cipher attacks under-perform on
sub-4B models because the models fail to decode the ciphers at all (a capability
artifact, not alignment), while DAN-family persona attacks remain effective.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Falsification conditions.} H1 is not supported if Kendall's
$\tau \ge 0.5$ for every judge pair under both conditions \emph{and} no pairwise
flip meets the \S5.7 rule. H2 is not supported if any model shows no significant
ASR reduction (McNemar $p \ge 0.05$) \emph{or} the max/min $C(M)$ ratio (among
models with a defined $C(M)$) is $\le 2$. H3 is not supported if the cipher-row
ASR is not uniformly low across models, or if low cipher ASR coincides with
measured cipher-decoding capability (i.e., the models \textit{can} decode but
still refuse --- indicating alignment, not capability).

\textbf{Exploratory (not confirmatory).} Per-category attack-family breakdowns
beyond the seven HarmBench semantic categories; qualitative cipher-decoding
analysis; any post-hoc judge-calibration adjustment; the placement ablation; the
GCG-suffix transfer magnitude.

\section{Metrics}
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
\item \textbf{Primary metric.} ASR on HarmBench-200, per judge, per (model,
condition).
\item \textbf{Secondary metrics.} FRR on XSTest-250 and OR-Bench-Hard
($n=1319$), measured with two judges (keyword + Llama-Guard-3-1B; the HarmBench
classifier is not used for FRR --- see paper \S4.6 / Appendix~F). Defense cost
$C(M) = (\mathrm{FRR}_{\mathrm{def}} - \mathrm{FRR}_{\mathrm{empty}}) /
(\mathrm{ASR}_{\mathrm{empty}} - \mathrm{ASR}_{\mathrm{def}})$; area metric
$A(M)$; the $(\Delta\mathrm{ASR}, \Delta\mathrm{FRR})$ pair per model.
\item Generation errors (backend exception / OOM / timeout) are a third outcome
state, excluded from both ASR and FRR denominators; per-cell error rate is
reported.
\end{itemize}

\section{Analysis plan (locked)}
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
\item \textbf{Point estimates.} Wilson 95\% score intervals (closed form) on
every single rate.
\item \textbf{Within-model before/after defense.} Exact-binomial McNemar test on
discordant pairs + paired nonparametric bootstrap ($B = 1000$) on the rate
difference.
\item \textbf{Cross-judge ranking stability.} Kendall's $\tau$ on model ASR
rankings for every pair of judges, per condition (three pairs for three judges).
\item \textbf{Cross-benchmark FRR.} Spearman correlation between XSTest and
OR-Bench-Hard FRR per model.
\item \textbf{Ranking-flip rule (H1).} A pairwise model ordering ``flips''
between two judges if the per-judge ASR point estimates change sign on at least
one pair \textbf{and} the Wilson 95\% CIs of the two judges' ASR estimates for
the affected pair do not overlap on the implied direction. Kendall's
$\tau < 0.5$ across judges is a secondary flip indicator.
\item \textbf{Defense-cost reporting rule (H2).} $C(M)$ is reported as
``undefined'' whenever the denominator's bootstrap 95\% CI includes zero; in
that case $A(M)$ and the $(\Delta\mathrm{ASR}, \Delta\mathrm{FRR})$ pair are the
substitute signals, plus a rank-based Pareto-dominance test.
\end{enumerate}
The analysis is implemented in \texttt{06\_analysis.py}; its expected output
schema (\texttt{metrics\_with\_ci.csv}, \texttt{paired\_tests.csv},
\texttt{pareto.csv}, \texttt{ranking\_stability.json}, \texttt{deltas.csv},
\texttt{summary.json}) is part of the tagged registration commit.

\section{Stopping / data-collection rules}
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
\item The harness halts and surfaces the underlying exception if any (model,
condition, benchmark) cell exceeds a 2\% generation-error rate, rather than
silently coding errors as refusals.
\item No optional-stopping on results: the full pre-registered grid is run to
completion before any hypothesis is evaluated.
\item No exclusion of prompts post hoc except the documented generation-error
state.
\end{itemize}
